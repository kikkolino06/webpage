League format

  • 4 teams: you and three computer GMs.
  • 6-round snake draft. Rosters are 3 F, 2 D and 1 G.
  • The computer GMs draft for value and roster need, with a little randomness, so no two drafts go the same way.
  • Weeks 1 to 9 are the regular season. You face each opponent three times.
  • Week 10 is the final between the top two teams.
  • Your league saves in this browser, so you can close the tab and come back later.

Hockey 101

New to hockey? Start here

Each team has six players on the ice at a time: three forwards, two defensemen and a goalie. That's the same shape as your fantasy roster.

F / C, LW, RW

Forwards

The center takes faceoffs and covers the middle. The wingers work the sides. Forwards score most of the goals, so they carry most fantasy teams.

D / LD, RD

Defensemen

They protect the net, block shots and move the puck up ice. Puck-moving defensemen like Cale Makar or Quinn Hughes rack up assists too.

G

Goalie

The last line of defense. A goalie on a strong team wins a lot. A goalie on a busy team makes a lot of saves. Both can pay off in fantasy.

Draft tips

There are only nine goalies in the pool and four teams need one. If two go early, don't wait long.

Blocked shots count here, so a shot-blocking defenseman can outscore a forward who doesn't shoot much.

Goals against cost a point each. A shaky goalie can sink an otherwise good week.

Weekly results are random, the same as real hockey. Judge your draft over the full season, not one week.
