How the NHL season works

From the first faceoff in October to the Stanley Cup Final in June.

Conferences and divisions

The 32 teams are split into two conferences of 16, and each conference into two divisions of eight.

Regular season

Starting in 2026-27, each team plays 84 games, 42 at home and 42 on the road, from October to mid-April. Teams face division rivals most often, but every team visits every arena at least once a season.

A game is three 20-minute periods. Teams earn points in the standings, not just wins:

ResultPoints
Win (regulation, overtime or shootout)2
Loss in overtime or a shootout1
Loss in regulation0

A game tied after 60 minutes goes to a five-minute, sudden-death overtime played three-on-three. If nobody scores, a shootout decides it. When two teams finish level on points, the first tiebreaker is regulation wins.

Playoffs

Sixteen teams make it, eight from each conference:

  • The top three teams in each division qualify automatically.
  • The next two best teams in each conference, regardless of division, get the wild cards.
  • Each division winner plays a wild card. The second and third place teams in each division play each other.

Every round is best-of-seven, and there are four rounds: two inside the division bracket, the conference final, then the Stanley Cup Final between the East and West champions. That's 16 wins to lift the Cup.

Playoff overtime is different. There's no shootout and no three-on-three. Teams play full 20-minute periods at five-on-five until someone scores, which is how some games run into a fourth or fifth overtime.

The draft and the salary cap

The NHL Draft is held every June and runs seven rounds. Teams that missed the playoffs enter a lottery for the top picks.

Each team's payroll has to stay under a hard salary cap, set at $104 million for 2026-27. There's also a floor, so teams can't spend too little either.
