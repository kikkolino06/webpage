Rules

Hockey rules in plain English

Enough to follow any game on TV, plus the awards the league hands out every June.

Positions

Each team has six players on the ice at a time: three forwards, two defensemen and a goalie. That's the same shape as your fantasy roster.

F / C, LW, RW

Forwards

The center takes faceoffs and covers the middle. The wingers work the sides. Forwards score most of the goals, so they carry most fantasy teams.

D / LD, RD

Defensemen

They protect the net, block shots and move the puck up ice. Puck-moving defensemen like Cale Makar or Quinn Hughes rack up assists too.

G

Goalie

The last line of defense. A goalie on a strong team wins a lot. A goalie on a busy team makes a lot of saves. Both can pay off in fantasy.

Offside

An attacking player can't cross the other team's blue line before the puck does. If they do, the play stops and there's a faceoff outside the zone.

Icing

If a team shoots the puck from its own half all the way past the other team's goal line and nobody touches it, that's icing. The faceoff comes back to the offending team's end, and they can't change players.

Penalties

A minor penalty, like tripping or hooking, sends a player to the box for two minutes. A major, usually for fighting, is five minutes. A misconduct is ten.

Power play

While a player sits in the box, their team plays short-handed and the other team is on the power play. If they score during a minor, the penalty ends early.

Pulling the goalie

A team that's trailing late often takes its goalie off for an extra skater. It raises the chance of tying the game, and also of giving up an empty-net goal.

Hat trick

Three goals by one player in a game. Fans throw hats on the ice to celebrate it.
